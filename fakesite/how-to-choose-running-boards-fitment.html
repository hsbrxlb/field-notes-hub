How to Choose Running Boards: Fitment, Length, and Step Height

Compare running board fitment, cab-length and wheel-to-wheel layouts, step height, and mounting kits. Know what to ask before ordering for your truck.

Choose running boards in two passes. First, match the truck and mounting kit. Then compare where your foot will land when you enter the cab or reach into the bed. A board can fit the truck and still put the step in the wrong place for you.

Identify the truck before comparing boards

Write down the year, make, model, exact cab name, and bed length. Include any body-generation designation used in the listing, plus existing side steps or rock rails.

Keep cab type, door count, and bed length separate. The cab identifies the passenger compartment; door count helps describe it; bed length identifies the cargo box. “Four-door truck” is too little information to choose an application. Match the manufacturer's cab name, such as SuperCab or SuperCrew. Ford's F-Series board listing, for example, specifies SuperCrew applications.

If you do not know your cab type, check your original window sticker or ask a dealer to identify the configuration from your VIN. Use that name in the product selector and the written fitment details.

Bed length deserves its own check, especially for boards extending behind the cab. Westin's wheel-to-wheel application chart lists different part numbers for some combinations of the same model and cab with different beds. Read the row and its footnotes for your truck; a matching cab alone does not settle that choice.

Compare length and step height separately

Cab-length and wheel-to-wheel describe how far a board runs along the truck. A dropped step describes a lower place to put your foot. Those are separate dimensions: choosing a longer board does not tell you whether entry will be easier.

Cab-length boards serve the door area. Wheel-to-wheel boards continue behind the cab toward the rear wheel opening, but some have no extra bed step. Confirm that there is a surface intended for standing where you need bed access. RealTruck's length comparison illustrates that distinction.

Use your main reason for buying to narrow the options:

What you need What to compare first What to confirm on the exact product
A step for getting into the cab Cab-length options Stepping areas beneath the doors you use, including the rear doors
Access to a toolbox near the front of the bed Wheel-to-wheel options with a bed step A usable step behind the cab, positioned near the toolbox
A lower first step into a tall truck Dropped-step layouts in the required length Installed step height and usable tread dimensions
Both easier entry and bed access Products with lower door steps and a bed step Separate confirmation of the door steps and the bed-access step

For step height, ask for the vertical distance from a stated vehicle reference point to the tread. Also ask about height above the ground on a truck with your suspension and tire setup. An overall product dimension or a pedal-width measurement cannot answer that question.

If you drive rutted trails, compare the lowest fixed part of each layout and the clearance it would leave on your truck.

The Dual Convex Platform option on OEDRO's Gladiator listing is an example of a two-level layout. That description alone does not establish wheel-to-wheel length or the installed height of either tread.

Compare usable tread width and depth, particularly if you wear work boots. If easier entry is the main goal, try an installed example when possible. Check that the person who needs the step can reach it comfortably and then reach the cab floor.

Check what mounts to the truck and what comes in the box

Open the installation instructions linked to the exact part number. Compare their parts list with the package contents, including the vehicle-specific brackets and hardware. Ask which pieces are included and which must be purchased separately. Westin's molded running boards, for example, require a separately sold vehicle-specific mounting kit.

Read the preparation steps before ordering. The SKU6752 installation guide linked from OEDRO's Gladiator listing requires factory rock rail removal for the step bar it covers. The listing has multiple styles, so ask the seller to match your selected style and bracket kit to the correct guide. If keeping your existing rails matters, resolve compatibility before choosing the boards.

For each candidate, check the instructions for drilling, trimming, accessory removal, and mounting locations. Use the requirements for that item and vehicle combination.

Resolve missing or conflicting details before ordering

Pause the purchase if the selector and written fitment disagree, the instructions appear to cover another part, or the photos show a different cab or board style. Request documentation for the exact option you intend to buy. A representative photo cannot settle its dimensions or bracket fit.

Send the seller a specific question:

I have a [year, make, model], [exact cab], with a [bed length] bed and [existing rails or other relevant modifications]. I'm considering [product link, selected style, and part number]. Please confirm this exact application, the included bracket kit, and the matching installation instructions. I need [rear-door access, bed access, or a lower entry step]. Can you provide a dimensioned drawing or a photo of this version installed on the same configuration? [Describe the missing detail or contradiction.] Which information applies to this part?

If the reply only says it fits, follow up on the unanswered detail. Keep the option unconfirmed until you have enough information to make the comparison.
