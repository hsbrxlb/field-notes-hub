How to Choose Running Boards: Fitment, Reach, and Step Layout

Compare cab-length and extended steps, tread placement, installed height, and mounting kits before choosing running boards.

Choose running boards around the door you use and the foot placement you need. The correct mounting kit gets the boards onto the truck; tread position and installed height determine whether they help you get in.

Start with the manufacturer's cab name

Record the year, model, exact cab name, and any body-generation designation used in the application. Include bed length when a board extends behind the cab. Cab type identifies the passenger compartment. Bed size describes the cargo box. They are separate fields, and neither should be guessed from the other.

“Four-door truck” is incomplete. Use a manufacturer name such as SuperCab or SuperCrew, then compare it with the application. Ford's M-16450-FSORB listing specifies SuperCrew vehicles. That is evidence for that Ford part, and a useful example of why door count alone cannot select an OEDRO bracket kit.

If you cannot identify the cab, use your original vehicle paperwork or ask a dealer to confirm it from the VIN. Photograph the entire side of the truck so a fitment inquiry shows the doors and the space between the cab and rear wheel.

Choose the places where you need a step

For everyday entry, look for a usable tread beneath each door that passengers use. An attractive raised section at the center of a board may do little for someone climbing through the rear door. Study installed photos of the same cab, with enough of the door opening visible to judge where a foot would land.

If the job is reaching a toolbox near the front of the bed, look for a designated bed-access step behind the cab. Overall board length does not prove that the rear section is intended for standing. Ask for a drawing that identifies the usable stepping surfaces and any limits on their use.

Wheel-to-wheel applications can depend on bed length. Westin's application chart assigns different versions to some cab-and-bed combinations. Treat that as a reason to read the exact application row, not a rule that every bed change requires different boards.

Compare installed height and usable tread

A dropped step and a longer board solve different problems. A dropped step changes the first foothold. An extended board changes how far the product reaches along the vehicle. OEDRO's Gladiator running-board page offers several platform styles; the style names alone cannot establish the installed height you need.

Ask for tread height measured from a clearly named vehicle reference point. If the seller gives a ground measurement, ask what tire and suspension setup it came from. Your lift or tire changes may make that measurement unsuitable for judging your own entry height.

Compare usable tread depth and width with the shoes you normally wear. Think through both movements: reaching the first step and moving from that step to the cab floor. If access is the reason for buying, an installed demonstration with the intended user is more useful than a photograph of someone else's boot.

For trail use, ask where the lowest fixed bracket or tread sits. A running board should not be treated as a rock slider or a jacking point unless its manufacturer specifically authorizes that use for the installed system.

Read the mounting-kit requirements

Check the package list and installation guide before purchase. You need the boards, the correct vehicle brackets, and their specified fasteners. Some products sell those items separately. Westin's molded-board range, for example, requires a separate vehicle-specific mounting kit. That does not describe every OEDRO package.

List factory steps, rock rails, mud flaps, or other equipment near the mounting area. Ask whether those parts stay in place. Read for drilling, trimming, and removal requirements. If the supplied instructions show a different platform layout from the one you selected, have the seller match the manual to the item before installation.

Make the final comparison concrete

For each finalist, write one sentence naming the exact vehicle and one naming the job: easier front-door entry, rear-passenger access, or reaching bed cargo. Underneath, record the confirmed tread position, installed height reference, mounting kit, and required changes.

A reply that says only “fits your truck” settles none of those usability questions. Ask for the missing dimension or photograph. For Ford-specific cab and interior distinctions that often accompany an accessory order, see the F-150 shopping guide.
